## Abstract

This report separates a mixed programming laboratory into three evidence classes: deterministic command-line utilities, artificial sensory abstractions, and artificial emotion models. The separation prevents ordinary calculators and speculative cognitive components from being evaluated under the same standard. Implemented modules are cataloged, architectural relationships are proposed, and all cognitive language is explicitly bounded: these programs model variables and rules, not consciousness or human-equivalent perception.

## Scope

Applied utilities, artificial sensory abstractions, and emotion-state prototypes.

Claims are limited to named repository artifacts. Proposed tests, benchmarks, integrations, and research directions are future work—not reported results.

## Reproducible inspection protocol

A reviewer should clone the repository, record the commit SHA and toolchain versions, inspect each mapped artifact, execute only examples with declared entry points, preserve outputs and errors, and compare observations with stated expectations. A deliberate debugging failure is evidence only when its expected failure class is declared in advance.

## Limitations

- The cognitive modules are early prototypes, not evidence of consciousness.
- The sensory modules do not yet process validated real-world multimodal datasets.
- Cross-module dynamics, tests, and measured evaluations remain future work.

## Development roadmap

- Define typed input/output contracts for every cognitive module.
- Extract Adaptive Memory Dynamics as a standalone research project.
- Create frozen behavioral scenarios before making learning or adaptation claims.
